Visual intuition · geometry before algebra

Same shape.
Different rules.

Imagine a square sticker on graph paper. A rigid transform lets you slide or rotate it. A similarity transform also lets you enlarge the entire sticker, like a photocopier. Every length gets the same multiplier, so the shape stays the same. Stretch only its width, and the square becomes a rectangle. Slide its top sideways relative to its bottom, and you create a shear.

In GigaPose, the template is a rendered image of the object. After the template supplies the approximate viewpoint, the method estimates a similarity map between image coordinates. Its four controls are one rotation angle, one uniform scale, and two translation coordinates. The rule is xq = sR xt + t. This image map is an approximation for real 3D objects under perspective; it is not the full 3D pose matrix. GigaPose §3.3, Eq. 4.

How the transform families fit together

Rigid ⊂ similarity ⊂ affine ⊂ projective, for the invertible maps here. A general nonlinear warp need not preserve straight lines. Stretch and shear are examples within the affine family.

Why does the paper call this “affine”?

Every similarity map is also affine. A generic affine map has an arbitrary 2 × 2 matrix A and a translation t. GigaPose restricts that matrix to A = sR. Its two columns stay perpendicular and have equal lengths. That restriction excludes shear and unequal scale.

      [ s cos θ   −s sin θ   tₓ ]
M  =  [ s sin θ    s cos θ   tᵧ ]
      [    0          0      1 ]

The last coordinate is a homogeneous coordinate equal to 1. It is not depth. The matrix acts on [x, y, 1].

One useful distinction: a 3D move versus a 2D image change

A rigid object can look smaller when it moves farther from a camera. The object did not shrink. Its image did. Under a weak-perspective approximation, this image size change acts like uniform scale. Depth variation and a different viewpoint can produce more complex image changes.

The tilt switch demonstrates another distinction: the diagram camera can change the apparent angles on your screen. The reported measurements stay on the image plane.
