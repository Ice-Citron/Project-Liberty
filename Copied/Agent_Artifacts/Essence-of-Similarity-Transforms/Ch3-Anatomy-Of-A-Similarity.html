Anatomy of a Similarity

A similarity is a recipe with four numbers: turn by α, grow by s, slide by t. Feed it a circle and you get a circle. Add one more ingredient, shear or squash, and watch the circle break.

    The essence
    A similarity has four numbers: s, α, tx, ty. Nothing else. Every slider under "The four numbers" is one of them.
    The linear part is s times a rotation. Its columns stay perpendicular and equal in length. In symbols: AᵀA = s²I.
    The same test, seen through shapes: both singular values equal s. So a circle maps to a circle, never to an ellipse.
    Shear and anisotropy are the two ways to break it. Both make σ1 ≠ σ2. The circle becomes an ellipse, and the test fails.
    Translation is not linear. It moves the origin. So it needs the +t, or the third column of the homogeneous matrix.
    "In GigaPose, the mapping Mt→q from template coordinates xt to query coordinates xq is constrained to a similarity transform: a rigid rotation, uniform scaling, and translation without shearing." GigaPose also excludes reflections: det A > 0. A camera never mirrors a rigid object.
