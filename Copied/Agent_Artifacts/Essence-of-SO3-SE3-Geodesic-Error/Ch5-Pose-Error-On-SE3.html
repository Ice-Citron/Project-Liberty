1 · Move the model's origin from the plane's centre to its nose. The planes stay where they are. Does θ change? Does ‖Δt‖ change?
θ stays. ‖Δt‖ changes. Here θ is for both origins. ‖Δt‖ is at the centre and at the nose. Δt is the gap between the two origin markers. A rotated plane carries its nose somewhere else.
2 · Keep the origin. Move the camera instead. Do the errors change?
No. θ stays at , ‖Δt‖ at and ‖ρ‖ at . The camera's motion multiplies both poses on the left, and it cancels. These errors are left-invariant. No metric on SE(3) can ignore both the camera and the origin. That is Park's result.
3 · Which L makes one degree cost the same as one millimetre?
mm. One degree is π/180 radians, so L·π/180 must equal 1 mm. With L = 0 the rotation is free. With a huge L the translation hardly matters.

Experiment 3 · the exchange rate L
To merge the two rulers, price one radian in millimetres. That price is a length L. It is a lever arm. A point at distance L from the error axis moves about L·θ.
Walk from the true pose to the estimate
Two rulers: turn steadily while the origin slides straight. One screw: turn about one fixed axis and slide along it. Same start. Same end.

Two ways to score a pose
(a) two rulers ΔR = R_est · R_gtᵀ θ = arccos((tr ΔR − 1) / 2) rad e_t = ‖t_est − t_gt‖₂ mm (b) one screw ξ = Log(T_gt⁻¹ · T_est) = (ρ, ω) ‖ω‖ = θ d = √(‖ρ‖² + L² θ²) mm
Option (a) keeps both units. Option (b) needs the length L to convert radians to millimetres.

new origin at the model point c: Δt′ = Δt + (R_est − R_gt) · c
The extra term vanishes only when the two rotations agree. θ never contains c, so it stays.

The Essence
Two units, so no natural single number. The protractor reads radians. The tape measure reads millimetres. Nothing in SE(3) says how to add them.
Option one: report two numbers. Give the geodesic angle θ and the distance ‖t_est − t_gt‖. It is simple and it is the standard.
The camera cannot change them. The origin can. Moving the camera changes neither ruler. Moving the model's origin changes ‖Δt‖ whenever the orientation is off.
Option two: one number, but you must pick L. L is a lever arm. It says how far a point at distance L moves per radian. Then d = √(‖ρ‖² + L²θ²).
Park, 1995. No bi-invariant distance metric exists on SE(3). Every metric hides a length scale. So the choice of L is yours, and you must state it.
BOP's MSSD is the geometry-aware answer. It measures in millimetres how far the object's own surface points move. The shape supplies the lever arm, and no origin has to be chosen. See MSSD, Chapter 1.
