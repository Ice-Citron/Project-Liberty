Essence of BOP Pose Errors

How does the BOP benchmark decide that an estimated 6D pose is right? It uses three different rulers. In each chapter you break a pose on purpose and watch what that ruler says. Every number comes from one shared implementation that follows the official bop_toolkit.

MSSD Maximum Symmetry-Aware Surface Distance Tie a rubber band between every matching vertex of the true copy and the estimated copy. MSSD is the longest band. minS maxx ‖P̂x − P̄Sx‖ Ruler: millimetres, in 3D. Thinks like a gripper.
MSPD Maximum Symmetry-Aware Projection Distance Same bands. A lamp at the camera casts their shadows on the image. MSPD is the longest shadow. minS maxx ‖proj(P̂x) − proj(P̄Sx)‖ Ruler: pixels, in the image. Thinks like an AR overlay.
VSD Visible Surface Discrepancy Two depth photos vote pixel by pixel. Only visible pixels get a ballot. VSD is the share that disagrees. avgp ∈ V̂∪V̄ [ not ( p ∈ V̂∩V̄ and |D̂−D̄| < τ ) ] Ruler: fraction of visible surface. Thinks like the depth sensor.

Chapters

Chapter 1 · about 12 min MSSD: the worst point, after the best symmetry Flip a box by 180°. Spin a can. Tilt a long rod. Watch the longest rubber band, and watch the symmetry list rescue the poses that only look wrong on paper. max picks the one worst vertex; min over S picks the closest of the equally correct poses. Chapter 2 · about 10 min MSPD: the same question, asked in pixels Push the estimate 80 mm away from the camera. MSSD is furious. MSPD hardly notices. Then change the image resolution and see why r = w/640 exists. MSPD is blind along the viewing ray, and a millimetre costs more pixels up close. Chapter 3 · about 15 min · the subtle one VSD: compare only what the camera can see Step through the pipeline: render, visibility, vote, score. Hide the mug's handle, spin it 50°, and watch VSD stay at zero while MSSD fails. VSD is the failing share of visible pixels, so invisible differences cost nothing. Chapter 4 · about 10 min One pose, three verdicts Seven scenarios where the three rulers disagree, and the arithmetic that turns their verdicts into BOP's Average Recall. which ruler sees depth error, which sees occlusion, and why BOP averages all three.

How to use these pages

Drag to orbit the scene. Scroll to zoom. Open a Predict first card, commit to an answer, then press its button and check yourself. Every number in the Math tab is computed live from the picture. Blue is the ground-truth pose. Warm is the estimate. Red is the worst point. These build on Essence of Similarity Transforms; GigaPose reports exactly the AR score explained in Chapter 4.
