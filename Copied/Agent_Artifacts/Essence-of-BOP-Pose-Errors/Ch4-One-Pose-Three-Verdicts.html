Scenarios
Each scenario is one wrong pose. The rulers disagree, or they agree for different reasons. Read the caption for the mechanism.
Predict first
Which error almost ignores a 50 mm depth mistake?
MSPD. A band that points at the camera casts a very short shadow. It is not zero: the estimate looks slightly smaller, so outer points shift a few pixels. MSSD sees the full 50 mm. VSD fails every pixel until τ exceeds the gap.
Which error needs no list of symmetries, and why?
VSD. It compares only what is visible. Two poses that look the same give the same depth photo, so they score the same. Untick "symmetry-aware": MSSD and MSPD now reject the flipped box. VSD does not move.
Which error knows about occlusion?
Only VSD. It reads the test depth image, so hidden pixels get no ballot. MSSD and MSPD use every model vertex, visible or not. Untick the occluder and the handle pixels vote again.

The three rulers
e_MSSD = min max ‖ P̂x − P̄Sx ‖ [mm] S∈S_M x∈V_M e_MSPD = min max ‖ proj(P̂x) − proj(P̄Sx) ‖ [px] S∈S_M x∈V_M e_VSD = avg { 0 if p ∈ V̂∩V̄ and |D̂(p) − D̄(p)| < τ p∈V̂∪V̄ { 1 otherwise [share]
P̂ is the estimate. P̄ is the ground truth. S_M lists the symmetries of the model. V_M lists its vertices. D̂ and D̄ are rendered distance maps. V̂ and V̄ are the visible pixels, found with the test depth image.
From errors to AR
AR = (AR_VSD + AR_MSSD + AR_MSPD) / 3
A pose is correct for one setting when e < θ. Recall is the fraction of annotated instances with a correct pose. Each AR averages recall over its settings: ten θ for MSSD and MSPD, ten τ times ten θ for VSD. The ladders show this pose's share of each.
GigaPose and other BOP methods report exactly this AR.
Caveat. The maps here are 128 by 96 cells over a zoomed window. BOP renders full images. The idea is the same; small numbers differ.
Source: Hodaň et al., BOP Challenge 2020 on 6D Object Localization, Sec. 2.2 to 2.4; bop_toolkit pose_error.py and visibility.py
The Essence
Three rulers, one pose. MSSD measures millimetres in 3D. MSPD measures pixels in the image. VSD measures a share of visible surface.
MSSD asks: would a gripper succeed? It reports the longest rubber band. One bad corner is enough to fail.
MSPD asks: would an AR overlay look right? It reports the longest shadow. Depth mistakes along the viewing ray barely show.
VSD asks: could this sensor tell the difference? Only visible pixels get a ballot. It alone handles occlusion and ambiguity that nobody listed.
Each ruler needs something. MSSD and MSPD need the symmetry list S_M. VSD needs a depth image of the scene.
BOP averages all three. AR = (AR_VSD + AR_MSSD + AR_MSPD) / 3. A method cannot win by being good on one ruler only.
