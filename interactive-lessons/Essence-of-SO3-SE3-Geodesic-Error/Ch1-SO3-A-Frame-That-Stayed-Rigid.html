SO(3): A Frame That Stayed Rigid

Glue three unit sticks at right angles to make a corner bracket. A rotation is any way to hold it. Which 3×3 matrices describe a bracket that stayed rigid?

Presets
Three freedoms
Two sliders pick an axis. The third turns the bracket about it. That is every way to hold the bracket.
Break the bracket
A matrix can also stretch, bend or mirror the sticks. Then it is not a rotation. The bend is a shear, so the y stick also gets a little longer.
Six constraints, one sign
    9 numbers − 6 constraints = 3 degrees of freedom
    Order matters
    Each button starts at the identity. It makes two 90° turns about the world axes, one after the other.

    3 · R has 9 entries. How many can you choose freely?
    Three. RᵀR = I is six constraints, and 9 − 6 = 3. The three sliders are exactly those freedoms. Move them: all six checks stay green.
    Definition
    R ∈ SO(3) ⟺ RᵀR = I and det R = +1 R⁻¹ = Rᵀ
    "Special" means det = +1. "Orthogonal" means RᵀR = I. The 3 means 3×3.
    The matrix on screen: M = R · D
    R is the turn from the three sliders. D is the damage from "Break the bracket". D = I means no damage.
    The test: MᵀM next to I
    The diagonal of MᵀM holds the squared stick lengths. The off-diagonal holds the dot products. det = +1 alone is not enough: a bend keeps det at 1 and still fails.
    Undo is free: R · Rᵀ
    For the turn R, the transpose is the inverse. This product stays at I for every slider setting.
    Order matters
    Turns about fixed world axes multiply on the left. "x then y" is Ry Rx.
    Counting freedom
    det = +1 removes no freedom. Orthogonal matrices have det +1 or −1, and the sign only picks one of two pieces. So the count stays 9 − 6 = 3.

    The Essence
    Columns are landing spots. The three columns of R are where the x, y and z sticks point.
    RᵀR = I is six sentences. Three say "this stick is one unit long". Three say "these two sticks meet at a right angle".
    det R = +1 rules out the mirror image. Orthogonal matrices come in two separate pieces. SO(3) is the piece that contains the identity.
    9 − 6 = 3 degrees of freedom. Nine entries, six constraints. Two numbers pick an axis, one picks the angle.
    Undo is free. The inverse of a rotation is its transpose: R⁻¹ = Rᵀ. No division, no solving.
    Order matters. Rotations do not commute. "x then y" is a different pose from "y then x".
    Caveat
    SO(3) says which way an object points. It never says where the object is. Chapter 4 adds the place.
