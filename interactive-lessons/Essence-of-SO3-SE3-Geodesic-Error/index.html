Essence of Rotations and Rigid Motions

SO(3), SE(3), and what a geodesic error really measures. Each chapter gives you one physical picture, lets you break it on purpose, and only then writes the formula. Every number comes from one shared implementation that passes 30 unit tests.

SO(3) Special Orthogonal group · pure 3D rotations A rigid bracket of three unit sticks at right angles. Any way of holding the bracket is a rotation. The columns of R are where the sticks point. RᵀR = I, det R = +1 3 degrees of freedom. Says which way it points.
SE(3) Special Euclidean group · rigid-body motions A two-step instruction: turn the bracket, then carry it to its place. T = [ R t ; 0 1 ], p_cam = R p_obj + t 6 degrees of freedom. Says where it points and where it is.
Geodesic error Shortest path inside the group Stick tips live on a globe. Walk along the surface. Do not tunnel through it. The walk is one turn about one axis. θ = arccos( (Tr(R_est R_gtᵀ) − 1) / 2 ) One angle in [0, π]. Unchanged by any choice of frames.

Chapters

Chapter 1 · about 10 min SO(3): a frame that stayed rigid Stretch a stick, bend a stick, mirror a stick. Watch six constraints light up or fail, and see why a mirror image is orthogonal yet not a rotation. RᵀR = I is three unit lengths and three right angles; 9 − 6 = 3 freedoms; det +1 forbids the mirror. Chapter 2 · about 12 min One skewer, one twist: axis-angle, Exp and Log Every rotation is a point in a solid ball of radius π. Dial 350° and watch Log hand back 10° about the opposite axis. Tr R = 1 + 2 cos θ, because the skewer direction stays put and the other two directions each contribute cos θ. Chapter 3 · about 15 min · the centrepiece Geodesic error: the arc, not the chord Walk on the globe or dig the tunnel. The tunnel leaves the world of rotations half-way, and its length saturates while the arc keeps growing. the Frobenius distance is 2√2 sin(θ/2); the geodesic angle is bi-invariant. Chapter 4 · about 12 min SE(3): a rotation plus a place Follow one point from object coordinates to camera coordinates. Then turn the plane about its nose and watch t change during a "pure" rotation. t is where the object's origin lands; the inverse is (Rᵀ, −Rᵀt); composing turns the second translation. Chapter 5 · about 15 min Pose error on SE(3): two rulers, or one screw Move the model's origin and the translation error changes while nothing physical moves. Move the camera and nothing changes. Then pick a lever arm L. no metric on SE(3) is blind to both choices, so every single-number error hides a length scale.
