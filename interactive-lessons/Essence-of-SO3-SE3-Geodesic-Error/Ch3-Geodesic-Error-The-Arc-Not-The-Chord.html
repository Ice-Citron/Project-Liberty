The formula

ΔR is the single turn that carries the true frame onto the estimate. Its trace hides the angle: Tr ΔR = 1 + 2 cos θ. Chapter 2 shows why.

Numerics
Clamp (Tr ΔR − 1)/2 to [−1, 1] before arccos. Rounding can push it outside. Near θ = 0 the arccos form loses digits. The form atan2(‖vee(ΔR − ΔRᵀ)‖/2, (Tr ΔR − 1)/2) stays accurate.
Notation
Lowercase log returns a skew matrix. The ∨ operator turns it into a vector. Capital Log does both steps. So the ∨ in ‖Log(ΔR)∨‖ is harmless but redundant.
Some authors take the Frobenius norm of the skew matrix log ΔR. That gives √2·θ, not θ. Check which one a paper uses.
Symmetric objects
A symmetric object has several correct orientations. The error is the smallest θ over its symmetry group. That is the same min over S as MSSD in the BOP series.

The Essence
One angle. The geodesic error is the smallest single turn that carries the true frame onto the estimate. It lives in [0, π].
Arc versus tunnel. θ is the walk on the globe. The Frobenius distance is the tunnel through it: 2√2·sin(θ/2).
Small errors agree. For small θ the tunnel is about √2·θ. For large θ it saturates at 2√2, so it under-reports.
The tunnel leaves SO(3). A straight blend of two rotation matrices is not a rotation. Only the arc stays rigid all the way.
θ is bi-invariant. No choice of camera frame or object axes changes it. That makes it the natural ruler on SO(3).
Two ways to write ΔR. R_est R_gtᵀ and R_gtᵀ R_est give the same θ and the same physical axis. Only the coordinates differ.
Honest caveat
θ says nothing about position. Two poses can share an orientation and sit a metre apart. Chapter 5 handles the full pose.
