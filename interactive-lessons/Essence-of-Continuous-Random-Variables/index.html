Essence of Continuous Random Variables

A discrete variable puts probability on bars. A continuous variable spreads it out like sand along a line. The height of the sand is the density. Only an area of sand is a probability. Each chapter starts from a picture you can drag. The formula comes last.

pdf f(x) Probability density function · the height of the sand Probability per unit of x. It can be larger than 1. Its total area is 1. P(a < X ≤ b) = ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx A single point has probability 0. Only intervals count.
cdf F(x) Cumulative distribution function · the sand so far The running total of area from the far left up to x. It climbs from 0 to 1. F(x) = ∫₋∞ˣ f(u) du, f = F′ The steepest climb sits where the density is highest.
quantile Q(p) The cdf read backwards Ask where the running total reaches p. The median is where it reaches one half. Q(p) = F⁻¹(p) The 97.5% point of N(0, 1) is 1.96.

Chapters

Chapter 1 · about 12 min · start here Density: area is probability Shrink the bins of a histogram until the bars become a curve. Then squeeze U(0, 1) and watch the density pass 1. P(a < X ≤ b) = F(b) − F(a); a single point has probability 0; f is the slope of F. Chapter 2 · about 12 min Mean, variance and quantiles Slice the curve into thin strips and treat each strip as a pmf bar. Σ x p(x) turns into ∫ x f(x) dx. E(X) is the balance point; Var(aX + b) = a² Var(X); Q(p) solves F(x) = p. Chapter 3 · about 15 min Uniform and Exponential: flat, then memoryless Chop time into tiny slots and the Geometric wait turns into Exp(λ). Wait a while and the curve ahead looks exactly the same. P(X > x + s | X > s) = e^(−λx); the gaps of a Poisson process are Exp(λ). Chapter 4 · about 15 min · tables The Normal: standardise, then read the table Every Normal is the standard Normal with a new ruler. Practise exam-style questions with the slide's own Φ table. F_X(x) = Φ((x − μ)/σ); Φ(−z) = 1 − Φ(z); N(0, 4) has σ = 2. Chapter 5 · about 12 min The mgf: every moment on one washing line One function holds every moment. Differentiate at t = 0 to pull them out. Multiply two of them to add two independent variables. E[Xⁿ] = M⁽ⁿ⁾(0); the mgf of a sum of independent variables is the product of their mgfs.
