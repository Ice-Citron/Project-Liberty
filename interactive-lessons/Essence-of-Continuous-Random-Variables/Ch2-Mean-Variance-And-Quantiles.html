Predict first
Read the question. Commit to an answer. Then open the card and check.
1 · Exp(1) has mean 1. Is its median above or below 1?
Below, at ln 2 ≈ 0.693. Half the area sits left of 0.693. The long right tail pulls the mean up. It cannot pull the median.
2 · Six strips turn U(0, 6) into a fair die. Is the die's σ² bigger or smaller than 3?
Smaller: 35/12 ≈ 2.917. The die puts each strip's mass at the strip's centre. That removes the spread inside each strip. More strips close the gap.
3 · Y = 3X + 5. Does the peak of the pdf grow or shrink?
It shrinks to one third. The curve gets 3 times wider. The area must stay 1. So fY(y) = fX((y − 5)/3) / 3.
4 · U(0, 1) has σ² = 1/12. What is σ² for U(0, 2)?
4/12 = 1/3. U(0, 2) is 2X with X ~ U(0, 1). Var(aX + b) = a²Var(X). So σ² grows by 2² = 4.
5 · What is the 90th percentile of Exp(1)?
ln 10 ≈ 2.303. Solve 1 − e−x = 0.9. So e−x = 0.1 and x = ln 10.
Each number is rounded to 4 significant figures. The sums use full precision. So a last digit can differ by 1.
Σ becomes ∫
discrete continuous p(x) f(x) dx Σ p(x) = 1 ∫ f(x) dx = 1 μ = Σ x p(x) μ = ∫ x f(x) dx E(g(X)) = Σ g p E(g(X)) = ∫ g(x) f(x) dx σ² = Σ (x−μ)² p σ² = ∫ (x − μ)² f(x) dx
The slide "Contrast with discrete random variable cdfs" gives the bridge. For small h the strip [x, x + h) holds probability about f(x)h. So each strip acts like one pmf bar. Thin strips turn each sum into an integral.
Linearity in one line
E(aX + b) = ∫ (ax + b) f(x) dx = a ∫ x f(x) dx + b ∫ f(x) dx = aμ + b · 1
The integral splits because integrals are linear. The second integral is the total area 1. The slide "Linearity of Expectation" states this result.
The variance shortcut
σ² = ∫ (x − μ)² f(x) dx = ∫ x² f dx − 2μ ∫ x f dx + μ² ∫ f dx = E(X²) − 2μ·μ + μ²·1 = E(X²) − μ²
Expand the square. Then use linearity three times.
(aX + b) − (aμ + b) = a(X − μ) Var(aX + b) = E(a²(X − μ)²) = a² Var(X)
The shift b cancels. So only the stretch a matters. It enters squared.
The pdf of Y = aX + b
a > 0: F_Y(y) = P(aX + b ≤ y) = F_X((y − b)/a) f_Y(y) = f_X((y − b)/a) / a a < 0: the inequality flips, F_Y = 1 − F_X((y − b)/a) f_Y(y) = f_X((y − b)/a) / |a|
This is a standard result. It uses the cdf method from the slide "Relationship between U(a, b) and U(0,1)". The factor 1/|a| keeps the area at 1.
Quantiles
Q(p) = F⁻¹(p), the least x with P(X ≤ x) = p median = Q(0.5) solves F(x) = 0.5 lower quartile = Q(0.25) = 25th percentile upper quartile = Q(0.75) = 75th percentile kth percentile = Q(k/100)
F climbs from 0 to 1. So each height p meets the curve once for these shapes. Read across from p and then down to x.
The slide's U(a, b) derivation
E(X) = ∫ₐᵇ x/(b − a) dx = [x²/(2(b − a))]ₐᵇ = (b² − a²)/(2(b − a)) = (b − a)(b + a)/(2(b − a)) = (a + b)/2 E(X²) = (b³ − a³)/(3(b − a)) = (a² + ab + b²)/3 Var(X) = (a² + ab + b²)/3 − (a + b)²/4 = (b − a)²/12

The Essence
Σ becomes ∫. A strip of width h holds probability f(x)h. So every pmf formula survives with p(x) replaced by f(x) dx.
μ is still the balance point. The pulls (x − μ)f(x) dx on the two sides cancel to 0.
σ² still averages squared distance. E((X − μ)²) and E(X²) − μ² always agree.
aX + b moves μ and scales σ by |a|. The pdf gets |a| times wider and |a| times shorter. The area stays 1.
A quantile runs the cdf backwards. Q(p) is the x with area p to its left.
The mean follows the long tail. The median does not. So they split apart on skewed shapes.
Mental-picture checklist
Honest caveats
Not every pdf has a mean. E(X) needs ∫ |x| f(x) dx to be finite. A Pareto pdf with α ≤ 1 fails this test. Chapter 1 meets the Pareto pdf.
The strips use midpoints. Other rules give slightly different sums. The exponential tail past the last strip enters as one exact piece.
The slide picks the least x with F(x) = p. This matters when F is flat over a gap. The four shapes here have no gaps.
"Mean right of median means right skew" is only a rule of thumb. The skewness page measures skew properly.
Sample quartiles follow the slide's ordered-data rule. Software packages interpolate in several different ways.
