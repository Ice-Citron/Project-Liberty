The formula
e_VSD = average over p ∈ V̂ ∪ V̄ of 0 if p ∈ V̂ ∩ V̄ and |D̂(p) − D̄(p)| < τ 1 otherwise
D̂ and D̄ are distance maps: distance from the camera centre, not z-depth. They come from rendering the model at the estimate and at the true pose. An empty union gives e = 1.
p ∈ V̄ when D̄(p) > 0 and ( D̄(p) − D_I(p) ≤ δ or D_I(p) = 0 ) V̂ : the same test on D̂, plus every p ∈ V̄ with D̂(p) > 0 δ = 15 mm

Why two visibility rules
Suppose the estimate sits 40 mm behind the real surface. The plain test would call it "occluded by the real object", and it would escape the vote. So every pixel of V̄ where the estimate renders also joins V̂. There the distances get compared, and the pixel fails.

The Essence
VSD compares two rendered distance maps, pixel by pixel. It looks only where the object is visible in the test image.
A pixel votes "fine" when both poses show the object there and the distances agree within τ. A pixel seen in only one pose is not fine. eVSD is the share that is not fine.
Differences the camera cannot see cost nothing: a hidden handle, a symmetric turn, an occluded part. No symmetry list is needed. BOP: VSD "treats poses that are indistinguishable in shape … as equivalent".
The test depth image has one job. It decides which pixels are visible, with tolerance δ = 15 mm. It never enters the distance comparison.
Each vote is all-or-nothing, since 2017. So VSD measures how much of the visible surface is misaligned, not by how many millimetres.
VSD needs a depth image, and it does punish depth error. MSPD does neither. That is why BOP keeps both.
Honest caveats
Colour is ignored. Two poses that differ only in texture are the same pose to VSD. A small part is a small share: a wrong handle costs little, because the handle covers few pixels. This page votes on a 128 × 96 window. BOP votes on the full image, so its numbers are finer.
How BOP scores it
A pose is correct when eVSD < θ. ARVSD is the average recall over ten values of τ and ten values of θ. Then AR = (ARVSD + ARMSSD + ARMSPD) / 3.
