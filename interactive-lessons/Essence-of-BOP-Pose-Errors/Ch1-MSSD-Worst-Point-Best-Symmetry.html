Predict first
Read the question. Commit to an answer. Then open the card and test it.
The box is flipped by 180°. Symmetry-aware is on. Is MSSD large, or zero?
Zero. The flipped box looks identical. One allowed turn S relabels the true copy, and every band shrinks to nothing. Now switch "Symmetry-aware" off. The same pose costs more than 100 mm.
The rod is rolled by 8°. How does the longest band compare with the average band?
The longest is far above the average. The rod turns about its middle. Bands near the middle stay short. The tip travels furthest. A gripper meets the tip, not the average.
Spin the mug by 40°. Spin the can by 40°. Which one does MSSD punish?
Only the mug. Its handle breaks the symmetry, so S_M holds the identity alone. The can has a whole circle of allowed turns. One of them cancels the spin almost exactly.
The formula
e_MSSD(P̂, P̄, S_M, V_M) = min max ‖ P̂x − P̄Sx ‖₂ S ∈ S_M x ∈ V_M
Read it inside out. Inside: for one allowed turn S, take the longest band. Outside: try every allowed turn and keep the smallest result. P̂ (P-hat) is the estimated pose. P̄ (P-bar) is the true pose. V_M holds the model vertices. S_M holds the turns that leave the model looking the same.

The Essence
MSSD is the longest rubber band. Tie every model vertex at the estimated pose to the same vertex at the true pose. The longest band, in millimetres, is the error.
Max, not mean. One bad corner is enough to fail. BOP says the maximum surface deviation "strongly indicates the chance of a successful grasp".
The max depends less on mesh density. Re-mesh a surface finer and the mean (ADD) shifts. The longest band barely moves.
min over S. A symmetric object has several correct poses. MSSD compares against the closest one. For a continuous symmetry, BOP samples turns so the furthest vertex moves at most 1% of the diameter per step.
One S for the whole object. ADI matches each vertex to its nearest neighbour separately. That can forgive poses that are really wrong. MSSD keeps every vertex tied to its own partner.
Correct means e_MSSD < θ. AR_MSSD averages recall over θ = 5% to 50% of the object diameter. Caveat: MSSD knows nothing about visibility or occlusion. Chapter 3 covers that.
