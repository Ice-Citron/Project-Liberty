The formula
e_MSPD = min max d(x, S) S∈S_M x∈V_M d(x,S) = ‖proj(P̂x) − proj(P̄Sx)‖₂ [px] proj(X,Y,Z) = (cx + f·X/Z, cy − f·Y/Z)
P̂ is the estimate. P̄ is the ground truth. S runs over the symmetries of the model. x runs over its vertices. It is the MSSD skeleton. Only the ruler changes: proj() turns each end of a band into a pixel.
Live numbers
Rule of thumb: f · dx / Z
The rule is exact only for a point at depth Z under a pure sideways slip. Vertices nearer than Z move a little more.

The Essence
Same rubber bands as MSSD. MSPD measures their shadows on the image, in pixels. The score is the longest shadow.
A band that points at the lamp casts almost no shadow. So error along the viewing ray is nearly invisible. BOP says MSPD "does not evaluate the alignment along the optical (Z) axis and measures only the perceivable discrepancy".
It is the ruler for augmented reality. A viewer sees the overlay, not the depth. It is also fair to RGB-only methods, which struggle to estimate depth from one image.
Near objects cost more pixels. The same millimetre error gives about f·dx/Z pixels. Halve Z and the shadow doubles.
Thresholds are 5r to 50r pixels, with r = w/640. Double the resolution and both the error and the thresholds double. The verdict stays.
Symmetries work exactly as in MSSD. For each S in S_M, find the longest shadow. Then keep the smallest. MSPD picks its own best S.
Honest caveats
MSPD alone cannot certify a pose for grasping. A large depth error passes. "Nearly invisible" is not "zero". A depth error rescales the object, so outer points still move a few pixels. That is why BOP averages MSPD with MSSD and VSD.
