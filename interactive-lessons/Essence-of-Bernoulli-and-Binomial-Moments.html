0 · The four formulas

L03 slides 10, 12, 15 and 16

Everything below uses only these four formulas. The rest is algebra.

The mean is a weighted average. Each value x gets the weight p(x). Picture the pmf bars as weights on a ruler. The mean is the point where the ruler balances.

1 · Bernoulli(p)

L03 slide 28 · claim: μ = p and σ² = p(1 − p)

X is 1 with probability p and 0 otherwise. The slide packs both cases into one formula: p(x) = px(1 − p)1−x. Put in x = 0 and x = 1 to unpack it.

The curve is p(1 − p) for every p. The dot is your current p.
Predict first: what is E(X²) for a Bernoulli?

It equals p. X only takes the values 0 and 1. Squaring leaves both unchanged, so X² = X.

Predict first: which p gives the largest variance?

p = ½ gives p(1 − p) = ¼. A fair coin is the hardest result to predict. Near 0 or 1 the result is almost certain. So the spread falls towards 0.

The mean doubles and the variance doubles. The SD grows only by √2 ≈ 1.41. Try n = 10 and then n = 20 at p = 0.25.

4 · Route 2: algebra from the pmf

L03 slide 31 says only “use simple combinatorial arguments”

Here are those arguments in full. Every line shows its own live value. All lines in a block must agree.

The one identity

Count teams of x people with one captain, from n people. One way: choose the team in ways. Then choose its captain in x ways. Another way: choose the captain first in n ways. Then fill the other x − 1 places from the n − 1 people left.

The mean

The x in front of each term blocks the Binomial Theorem. The identity swaps it for a factor n and a smaller coefficient. The leftover sum is the pmf of Binomial(n − 1, p). Any pmf adds to 1.

The variance

E(X²) puts x² in front. The identity removes only one factor of x. So split x² = x(x − 1) + x. The product x(x − 1) cancels the top two factors of x! = x(x − 1)(x − 2)!. First find E[X(X − 1)] the same way as the mean.

5 · Route 3: a sum of Bernoullis

L03 slides 24 and 26 · slide 31 says “from pmf or results on sums of r.vs.”

Write X = X₁ + X₂ + … + Xn. Each Xi is 1 if trial i succeeds and 0 otherwise. So each Xi is Bernoulli(p) from section 1. This is the shortest route.

6 · Takeaways

  • The mean is the balance point of the pmf bars.
  • For a Bernoulli, X² = X. So E(X²) = p and Var(X) = p − p² = p(1 − p).
  • In the Binomial sum, the identity removes the x in front.
  • The leftover sum is a smaller Binomial pmf. It adds to 1.
  • For the variance, find E[X(X − 1)] first. Then use E(X²) = E[X(X − 1)] + E(X).
  • Route 3 is the fastest. The mean rule needs no independence, but the variance rule does.

Honest caveats
