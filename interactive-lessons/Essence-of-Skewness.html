Skewness: the cubed seesaw

The mean is the point where the bars balance. Now let every distance from the mean count cubed. Which way does the seesaw tip, and how hard? Measured in units of σ³, that tip is the skewness γ₁.

Predict first
Read the question. Commit to an answer. Then open the card and check.
1 · A fair die. Is γ₁ positive, negative or zero?
Zero. Each deviation from 3.5 has a twin of the opposite sign. Cubes keep the sign. So the pairs cancel.
2 · Bernoulli(0.1) has its tall bar on the left. Which way does it skew?
Right, γ₁ = +2.67. The tall bar is close to μ = 0.1. The rare 1 is 0.9 away. Distance is cubed but probability is not. A long lever beats a heavy weight.
3 · Poisson: raise λ from 1 to 25. What happens to γ₁?
It shrinks like 1/√λ. It falls from 1 to 0.2. The bulk moves away from the wall at 0. The pmf looks more and more symmetric.
4 · Stretch X by 3. Does γ₁ change? What if you flip X?
Stretch: no change. E[(X − μ)³] grows by 3³ = 27. σ³ grows by 27 too. Flip: the sign changes. Every (x − μ) changes sign. So every cube does too.
5 · Can a lopsided pmf have γ₁ = 0?
Yes. Load the example and look at step 4. The left and right pulls have equal size. A symmetric pmf always has γ₁ = 0. The reverse is false.

The formula
γ₁ = E[(X − μ)³] / σ³ = Σₓ (x − μ)³ p(x) / σ³ = Σₓ z³ p(x), z = (x − μ) / σ shortcut, like Var(X) = E[X²] − μ²: E[(X − μ)³] = E[X³] − 3μ E[X²] + 2μ³
The last line of the definition is the picture. Turn each outcome into a z-score. Cube it. Average with the pmf weights.

Lecture closed forms
Bernoulli, by hand: E[(X − p)³] = (1 − p)(0 − p)³ + p(1 − p)³ = p(1 − p)[(1 − p)² − p²] = p(1 − p)(1 − 2p) σ³ = [p(1 − p)]^(3/2) γ₁ = (1 − 2p) / √(p(1 − p))
Why Binomial divides by √n. Take independent X and Y. Call their centred parts A and B. Then E[(A + B)³] = E[A³] + 3E[A²]E[B] + 3E[A]E[B²] + E[B³]. E[A] and E[B] are 0. So third central moments add, just as variances do. n Bernoullis give n·p(1 − p)(1 − 2p) on top and [np(1 − p)]^(3/2) below.

The Essence
Same recipe, third power. The mean makes Σ(x − μ)p(x) vanish. The variance averages (x − μ)². Skewness averages z³.
Cubing keeps the side. Bars left of μ pull negative. Bars right of μ pull positive.
Cubing rewards distance. Twice as far pulls 8 times as hard per unit of probability. Rare far values often decide γ₁.
The tail wins, not the hump. γ₁ points toward the longer tail. The tall bars often sit on the other side.
σ³ makes it a pure number. Shifting or stretching X leaves γ₁ unchanged. Flipping X flips its sign.
The lecture families. Bernoulli and Binomial take the sign of 1 − 2p. Binomial shrinks like 1/√n. Poisson gives 1/√λ. Geometric never drops below 2. Uniform gives 0.
Mental-picture checklist
Honest caveats
Zero skewness does not mean symmetry. Tour stop 15 shows a lopsided pmf with γ₁ = 0.
"Mean above median means right skew" is only a rule of thumb. It often fails for discrete pmfs.
Data sets use sample skewness. Software often adds a small-sample correction. This page uses the population definition from the lecture.
The Geometric here counts trials, as in the lecture. Some books count the failures before the first success. That shifts X by 1. So γ₁ stays the same.
The words slightly, moderately and strongly follow a common rule of thumb. The cut points are |γ₁| = 0.5 and |γ₁| = 1.
